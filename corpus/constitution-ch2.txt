<h2 class="page_s1"><a name="7" id="7"></a>7. Rights</h2>
<p>1. This Bill of Rights is a cornerstone of democracy in South Africa. It enshrines the rights of all people in our country and affirms the democratic values of human dignity, equality and freedom.</p>
<p>2. The state must respect, protect, promote and fulfil the rights in the Bill of Rights.</p>
<p>3. The rights in the Bill of Rights are subject to the limitations contained or referred to in section 36, or elsewhere in the Bill.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="8" name="8"></a>8. Application</h2>
<p>1. The Bill of Rights applies to all law, and binds<strong> </strong>the legislature, the executive, the judiciary and all organs of state.</p>
<p>2. A provision of the Bill of Rights binds a natural or a juristic person if, and to the extent that, it is applicable, taking into account the nature of the right and the nature of any duty imposed by the right.</p>
<p>3. When applying a provision of the Bill of Rights to a natural or juristic person in terms of subsection (2), a court ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. in order to give effect to a right in the Bill, must apply, or if necessary develop, the common law to the extent that legislation does not give effect to that right; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. may develop rules of the common law to limit the right, provided that the limitation is in accordance with section 36(1).</p>
<p>4. A juristic person is entitled to the rights in the Bill of Rights to the extent required by the nature of the rights and the nature of that juristic person.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="9" name="9"></a>9. Equality</h2>
<p>1. Everyone is equal before the law and has the right to equal protection and benefit of the law.</p>
<p>2. Equality includes the full and equal enjoyment of all rights and freedoms. To promote the achievement of equality, legislative and other measures designed to protect or advance persons, or categories of persons, disadvantaged by unfair discrimination may be taken.</p>
<p>3. The state may not unfairly discriminate directly or indirectly against anyone on one or more grounds, including race, gender, sex, pregnancy, marital status, ethnic or social origin, colour, sexual orientation, age, disability, religion, conscience, belief, culture, language and birth.</p>
<p>4. No person may unfairly discriminate directly or indirectly against anyone on one or more grounds in terms of subsection (3). National legislation must be enacted to prevent or prohibit unfair discrimination.</p>
<p>5. Discrimination on one or more of the grounds listed in subsection (3) is unfair unless it is established that the discrimination is fair.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="10" name="10"></a>10. Human dignity</h2>
<p>Everyone has inherent dignity and the right to have their dignity respected and protected.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="11" name="11"></a>11. Life</h2>
<p>Everyone has the right to life.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="12" name="12"></a>12. Freedom and security of the person</h2>
<p>1. Everyone has the right to freedom and security of the person, which includes the right ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. not to be deprived of freedom arbitrarily or without just cause;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. not to be detained without trial;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. to be free from all forms of violence from either public or private sources;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">d. not to be tortured in any way; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">e. not to be treated or punished in a cruel, inhuman or degrading way.<br>&nbsp;</p>
<p>2. Everyone has the right to bodily and psychological integrity, which includes the right ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to make decisions concerning reproduction;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to security in and control over their body; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. not to be subjected to medical or scientific experiments without their informed consent.<span id="cke_bm_560E" style="display: none;">&nbsp;</span></p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="13" name="13"></a>13. Slavery, servitude and forced labour</h2>
<p>No one may be subjected to slavery, servitude or forced labour.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="14" name="14"></a>14. Privacy</h2>
<p>Everyone has the right to privacy, which includes the right not to have ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. their person or home searched;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. their property searched;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. their possessions seized; or</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">d. the privacy of their communications infringed.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="15" name="15"></a>15. Freedom of religion, belief and opinion</h2>
<p>1. Everyone has the right to freedom of conscience, religion, thought, belief and opinion.</p>
<p>2. Religious observances may be conducted at state or state-aided institutions, provided that ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. those observances follow rules made by the appropriate public authorities;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. they are conducted on an equitable basis; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. attendance at them is free and voluntary.</p>
<p>3.&nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp;a. This section does not prevent legislation recognising ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">i. marriages concluded under any tradition, or a system of religious, personal or family law; or</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">ii. systems of personal and family law under any tradition, or adhered to by persons professing a particular religion.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. Recognition in terms of paragraph (a) must be consistent with this section and the other provisions of the Constitution.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="16" name="16"></a>16. Freedom of expression</h2>
<p>1. Everyone has the right to freedom of expression, which includes ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. freedom of the press and other media;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. freedom to receive or impart information or ideas;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. freedom of artistic creativity; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">d. academic freedom and freedom of scientific research.</p>
<p>2. The right in subsection (1) does not extend to ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. propaganda for war;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. incitement of imminent violence; or</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. advocacy of hatred that is based on race, ethnicity, gender or religion, and that constitutes incitement to cause harm.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="17" name="17"></a>17. Assembly, demonstration, picket and petition</h2>
<p>Everyone has the right, peacefully and unarmed, to assemble, to demonstrate, to picket and to present petitions.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="18" name="18"></a>18. Freedom of association</h2>
<p>Everyone has the right to freedom of association.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="19" name="19"></a>19. Political rights</h2>
<p>1. Every citizen is free to make political choices, which includes the right</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to form a political party;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to participate in the activities of, or recruit members for, a political party; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. to campaign for a political party or cause.<br>&nbsp;</p>
<p>2. Every citizen has the right to free, fair and regular elections for any legislative body established in terms of the Constitution.</p>
<p>3. Every adult citizen has the right ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to vote in elections for any legislative body established in terms of the Constitution, and to do so in secret; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to stand for public office and, if elected, to hold office.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="20" name="20"></a>20. Citizenship</h2>
<p>No citizen may be deprived of citizenship.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="21" name="21"></a>21. Freedom of movement and residence</h2>
<p>1. Everyone has the right to freedom of movement.</p>
<p>2. Everyone has the right to leave the Republic.</p>
<p>3. Every citizen has the right to enter, to remain in and to reside anywhere in, the Republic.</p>
<p>4. Every citizen has the right to a passport.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="22" name="22"></a>22. Freedom of trade, occupation and profession</h2>
<p>Every citizen has the right to choose their trade, occupation or profession freely. The practice of a trade, occupation or profession may be regulated by law.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="23" name="23"></a>23. Labour relations</h2>
<p>1. Everyone has the right to fair labour practices.</p>
<p>2. Every worker has the right ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to form and join a trade union;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to participate in the activities and programmes of a trade union; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. to strike.</p>
<p>3. Every employer has the right ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to form and join an employers' organisation; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to participate in the activities and programmes of an employers' organisation.</p>
<p>4. Every trade union and every employers' organisation has the right ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to determine its own administration, programmes and activities;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to organise; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. to form and join a federation.</p>
<p>5. Every trade union, employers' organisation and employer has the right to engage in collective bargaining. National legislation may be enacted to regulate collective bargaining. To the extent that the legislation may limit a right in this Chapter, the limitation must comply with section 36(1).</p>
<p>6. National legislation may recognise union security arrangements contained in collective agreements. To the extent that the legislation may limit a right in this Chapter, the limitation must comply with section 36(1).</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="24" name="24"></a>24. Environment</h2>
<p>Everyone has the right ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to an environment that is not harmful to their health or well-being; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to have the environment protected, for the benefit of present and future generations, through reasonable legislative and other measures that ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">i. prevent pollution and ecological degradation;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">ii. promote conservation; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">iii. secure ecologically sustainable development and use of natural resources while promoting justifiable economic and social development.<span id="cke_bm_845E" style="display: none;">&nbsp;</span></p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="25" name="25"></a>25. Property</h2>
<p>1. No one may be deprived of property except in terms of law of general application, and no law may permit arbitrary deprivation of property.</p>
<p>2. Property may be expropriated only in terms of law of general application ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. for a public purpose<strong> </strong>or in the public interest; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. subject to compensation, the amount of which and the time and manner of payment of which have either been agreed to by those affected or decided or approved by a court.</p>
<p>3. The amount of the compensation and the time and manner of payment must be just and equitable, reflecting an equitable balance between the public interest and the interests of those affected, having regard to all relevant circumstances, including ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. the current use of the property;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. the history of the acquisition and use of the property;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. the market value of the property;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">d. the extent of direct state investment and subsidy in the acquisition and beneficial capital improvement of the property; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">e. the purpose of the expropriation.<span id="cke_bm_909E" style="display: none;"> </span></p>
<p>4. For the purposes of this section ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. the public interest includes the nation's commitment to land reform, and to reforms to bring about equitable access to all South Africa's natural resources; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. property is not limited to land.</p>
<p>5. The state must take reasonable legislative and other measures, within its available resources, to foster conditions which enable citizens to gain access to land on an equitable basis.</p>
<p>6. A person or community whose tenure of land is legally insecure as a result of past racially discriminatory laws or practices is entitled, to the extent provided by an Act of Parliament, either to tenure which is legally secure or to comparable redress.</p>
<p>7. A person or community dispossessed of property after 19 June 1913 as a result of past racially discriminatory laws or practices is entitled, to the extent provided by an Act of Parliament, either to restitution of that property or to equitable redress.</p>
<p>8. No provision of this section may impede the state from taking legislative and other measures to achieve land, water and related reform, in order to redress the results of past racial discrimination, provided that any departure from the provisions of this section is in accordance with the provisions of section 36(1).</p>
<p>9. Parliament must enact the legislation referred to in subsection<strong> </strong>(6).</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="26" name="26"></a>26. Housing</h2>
<p>1. Everyone has the right to have access to adequate housing.</p>
<p>2. The state must take reasonable legislative and other measures, within its available resources, to achieve the progressive realisation of this right.</p>
<p>3. No one may be evicted from their home, or have their home demolished, without an order of court made after considering all the relevant circumstances. No legislation may permit arbitrary evictions.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="27" name="27"></a>27. Health care, food, water and social security</h2>
<p>1. Everyone has the right to have access to ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. health care services, including reproductive health care;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. sufficient food and water; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. social security, including, if they are unable to support themselves and their dependants, appropriate social assistance.</p>
<p>2. The state must take reasonable legislative and other measures, within its available resources, to achieve the progressive realisation of each of these rights.</p>
<p>3. No one may be refused emergency medical treatment.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="28" name="28"></a>28. Children</h2>
<p>1. Every child has the right ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to a name and a nationality from birth;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to family care or parental care, or to appropriate alternative care when removed from the family environment;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. to basic nutrition, shelter, basic health care services and social services;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">d. to be protected from maltreatment, neglect, abuse or degradation;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">e. to be protected from exploitative labour practices;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">f. not to be required or permitted to perform work or provide services that ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">i. are inappropriate for a person of that child's age; or</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">ii. place at risk the child's well-being, education, physical or mental health or spiritual, moral or social development;<span id="cke_bm_1011E" style="display: none;">&nbsp;</span></p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">g. not to be detained except as a measure of last resort, in which case, in addition to the rights a child enjoys under sections 12 and 35, the child may be detained only for the shortest appropriate period of time, and has the right to be ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">i. kept separately from detained persons over the age of 18 years; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">ii. treated in a manner, and kept in conditions, that take account of the child's age;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">h. to have a legal practitioner assigned to the child by the state, and at state expense, in civil proceedings affecting the child, if substantial injustice would otherwise result; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">i. not to be used directly in armed conflict, and to be protected in times of armed conflict.</p>
<p>2. A child's best interests are of paramount importance in every matter concerning the child.</p>
<p>3. In this section "child" means a person under the age of 18 years.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="29" name="29"></a>29. Education</h2>
<p>1. Everyone has the right ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to a basic education, including adult basic education; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to further education, which the state, through reasonable measures, must make progressively available and accessible.</p>
<p>2. Everyone has the right to receive education in the of ficial language or languages of their choice in public educational institutions where that education is reasonably practicable. In order to ensure the effective access to, and implementation of, this right, the state must consider all reasonable educational alternatives, including single medium institutions, taking into account ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. equity;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. practicability; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. the need to redress the results of past racially discriminatory laws and practices.</p>
<p>3. Everyone has the right to establish and maintain, at their own expense, independent educational institutions that ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. do not discriminate on the basis of race;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. are registered with the state; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. maintain standards that are not inferior to standards at comparable public educational institutions.</p>
<p>4. Subsection (3) does not preclude state subsidies for independent educational institutions.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="30" name="30"></a>30. Language and culture</h2>
<p>Everyone has the right to use the language and to participate in the cultural life of their choice, but no one exercising these rights may do so in a manner inconsistent with any provision of the Bill of Rights.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="31" name="31"></a>31. Cultural, religious and linguistic communities</h2>
<p>1. Persons belonging to a cultural, religious or linguistic community may not be denied the right, with other members of that community ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to enjoy their culture, practise their religion and use their language; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to form, join and maintain cultural, religious and linguistic associations and other organs of civil society.</p>
<p>2. The rights in subsection (1) may not be exercised in a manner inconsistent with any provision of the Bill of Rights.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1">&nbsp;<a id="32" name="32"></a>32. Access to information</h2>
<p>1. Everyone has the right of access to ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. any information held by the state; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. any information that is held by another person and that is required for the exercise or protection of any rights.</p>
<p>2. National legislation must be enacted to give effect to this right, and may provide for reasonable measures to alleviate the administrative and financial burden on the state.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1">&nbsp;<a id="33" name="33"></a>33. Just administrative action</h2>
<p>1. Everyone has the right to administrative action that is lawful, reasonable and procedurally fair.</p>
<p>2. Everyone whose rights have been adversely affected by administrative action has the right to be given written reasons.</p>
<p>3. National legislation must be enacted to give effect to these rights, and must ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. provide for the review of administrative action by a court or, where appropriate, an independent and impartial tribunal;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. impose a duty on the state to give effect to the rights in subsections (1) and (2); and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. promote an efficient administration.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="34" name="34"></a>34. Access to courts</h2>
<p>Everyone has the right to have any dispute that can be resolved by the application of law decided in a fair public hearing before a court or, where appropriate, another independent and impartial tribunal or forum.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="35" name="35"></a>35. Arrested, detained and accused persons</h2>
<p>1. Everyone who is arrested for allegedly committing an offence has the right ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to remain silent;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to be informed promptly ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">i. of the right to remain silent; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">ii. of the consequences of not remaining silent;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. not to be compelled to make any confession or admission that could be used in evidence against that person;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">d. to be brought before a court as soon as reasonably possible, but not later than ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">i. 48 hours after the arrest; or</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">ii. the end of the first court day after the expiry of the 48 hours, if the 48 hours expire outside ordinary court hours or on a day which is not an ordinary court day;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">e. at the first court appearance after being arrested, to be charged or to be informed of the reason for the detention to continue, or to be released; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">f. to be released from detention if the interests of justice permit, subject to reasonable conditions.</p>
<p>2. Everyone who is detained, including every sentenced prisoner, has the right ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to be informed promptly of the reason for being detained;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to choose, and to consult with, a legal practitioner, and to be informed of this right promptly;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. to have a legal practitioner assigned to the detained person by the state and at state expense, if substantial injustice would otherwise result, and to be informed of this right promptly;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">d. to challenge the lawfulness of the detention in person before a court and, if the detention is unlawful, to be released;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">e. to conditions of detention that are consistent with human dignity, including at least exercise and the provision, at state expense, of adequate accommodation, nutrition, reading material and medical treatment; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">f. to communicate with, and be visited by, that person's ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">i. spouse or partner;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">ii. next of kin;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">iii. chosen religious counsellor; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">iv. chosen medical practitioner.</p>
<p>3. Every accused person has a right to a fair trial, which includes the right ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. to be informed of the charge with sufficient detail to answer it;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. to have adequate time and facilities to prepare a defence;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. to a public trial before an ordinary court;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">d. to have their trial begin and conclude without unreasonable delay;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">e. to be present when being tried;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">f. to choose, and be represented by, a legal practitioner, and to be informed of this right promptly;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">g. to have a legal practitioner assigned to the accused person by the state and at state expense, if substantial injustice would otherwise result, and to be informed of this right promptly;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">h. to be presumed innocent, to remain silent, and not to testify during the proceedings;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">i. to adduce and challenge evidence;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">j. not to be compelled to give self-incriminating evidence;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">k. to be tried in a language that the accused person understands or, if that is not practicable, to have the proceedings interpreted in that language;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">l. not to be convicted for an act or omission that was not an offence under either national or international law at the time it was committed or omitted;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">m. not to be tried for an offence in respect of an act or omission for which that person has previously been either acquitted or convicted;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">n. to the benefit of the least severe of the prescribed punishments if the prescribed punishment for the offence has been changed between the time that the offence was committed and the time of sentencing; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">o. of appeal to, or review by, a higher court.</p>
<p>4. Whenever this section requires information to be given to a person, that information must be given in a language that the person understands.</p>
<p>5. Evidence obtained in a manner that violates any right in the Bill of Rights must be excluded if the admission of that evidence would render the trial unfair or otherwise be detrimental to the administration of justice.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="36" name="36"></a>36. Limitation of rights</h2>
<p>1. The rights in the Bill of Rights may be limited only in terms of law of general application to the extent that the limitation is reasonable and justifiable in an open and democratic society based on human dignity, equality and freedom, taking into account all relevant factors, including ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. the nature of the right;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. the importance of the purpose of the limitation;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. the nature and extent of the limitation;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">d. the relation between the limitation and its purpose; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">e. less restrictive means to achieve the purpose.</p>
<p>2. Except as provided in subsection (1) or in any other provision of the Constitution, no law may limit any right entrenched in the Bill of Rights.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="37" name="37"></a>37. States of emergency</h2>
<p>1. A state of emergency may be declared only in terms of an Act of Parliament, and only when ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. the life of the nation is threatened by war, invasion, general insurrection, disorder, natural disaster or other public emergency; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. the declaration is necessary to restore peace and order.</p>
<p>2. A declaration of a state of emergency, and any legislation enacted or other action taken in consequence of that declaration, may be effective only ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. prospectively; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. for no more than 21 days from the date of the declaration, unless the National Assembly resolves to extend the declaration. The Assembly may extend a declaration of a state of emergency for no more than three months at a time. The first extension of the state of emergency must be by a resolution adopted with a supporting vote of a majority of the members of the Assembly. Any subsequent extension must be by a resolution adopted with a supporting vote of at least 60 per cent of the members of the Assembly. A resolution in terms of this paragraph may be adopted only following a public debate in the Assembly.</p>
<p>3. Any competent court may decide on the validity of ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. a declaration of a state of emergency;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. any extension of a declaration of a state of emergency; or</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. any legislation enacted, or other action taken, in consequence of a declaration of a state of emergency.</p>
<p>4. Any legislation enacted in consequence of a declaration of a state of emergency may derogate from the Bill of Rights only to the extent that ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. the derogation is strictly required by the emergency; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. the legislation ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">i. is consistent with the Republic's obligations under international law applicable to states of emergency;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">ii. conforms to subsection (5); and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 80px;">iii. is published in the national Government Gazette as soon as reasonably possible after being enacted.</p>
<p>5. No Act of Parliament that authorises a declaration of a state of emergency, and no legislation enacted or other action taken in consequence of a declaration, may permit or authorise ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. indemnifying the state, or any person, in respect of any unlawful act;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. any derogation from this section; or</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. any derogation from a section mentioned in column 1 of the Table of Non-Derogable Rights, to the extent indicated opposite that section in column 3 of the Table.</p>

<p>6. Whenever anyone is detained without trial in consequence of a derogation of rights resulting from a declaration of a state of emergency, the following conditions must be observed:</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. An adult family member or friend of the detainee must be contacted as soon as reasonably possible, and informed that the person has been detained.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. A notice must be published in the national Government Gazette within five days of the person being detained, stating the detainee's name and place of detention and referring to the emergency measure in terms of which that person has been detained.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. The detainee must be allowed to choose, and be visited at any reasonable time by, a medical practitioner.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">d. The detainee must be allowed to choose, and be visited at any reasonable time by, a legal representative.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">e. A court must review the detention as soon as reasonably possible, but no later than 10 days after the date the person was detained, and the court must release the detainee unless it is necessary to continue the detention to restore peace and order.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">f. A detainee who is not released in terms of a review under paragraph (e), or who is not released in terms of a review under this paragraph, may apply to a court for a further review of the detention at any time after 10 days have passed since the previous review, and the court must release the detainee unless it is still necessary to continue the detention to restore peace and order.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">g. The detainee must be allowed to appear in person before any court considering the detention, to be represented by a legal practitioner at those hearings, and to make representations against continued detention.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">h. The state must present written reasons to the court to justify the continued detention of the detainee, and must give a copy of those reasons to the detainee at least two days before the court reviews the detention.</p>
<p>7. If a court releases a detainee, that person may not be detained again on the same grounds unless the state first shows a court good cause for re-detaining that person.<br>&nbsp;</p>
<p>8. Subsections (6) and (7) do not apply to persons who are not South African citizens and who are detained in consequence of an international armed conflict. Instead, the state must comply with the standards binding on the Republic under international humanitarian law in respect of the detention of such persons.</p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="38" name="38"></a>38. Enforcement of rights</h2>
<p>Anyone listed in this section has the right to approach a competent court, alleging that a right in the Bill of Rights has been infringed or threatened, and the court may grant appropriate relief, including a declaration of rights. The persons who may approach a court are -</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. anyone acting in their own interest;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. anyone acting on behalf of another person who cannot act in their own name;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. anyone acting as a member of, or in the interest of, a group or class of persons;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">d. anyone acting in the public interest; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">e. an association acting in the interest of its members.<span id="cke_bm_1741E" style="display: none;">&nbsp;</span></p>
<h2 class="page_s1"><a id="39" name="39"></a>39. Interpretation of Bill of Rights</h2>
<p>1. When interpreting the Bill of Rights, a court, tribunal or forum ­</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">a. must promote the values that underlie an open and democratic society based on human dignity, equality and freedom;</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">b. must consider international law; and</p>
<p style="margin-left: 40px;">c. may consider foreign law.</p>
<p>2. When interpreting any legislation, and when developing the common law or customary law, every court, tribunal or forum must promote the spirit, purport and objects of the Bill of Rights.</p>
<p>3. The Bill of Rights does not deny the existence of any other rights or freedoms that are recognised or conferred by common law, customary law or legislation, to the extent that they are consistent with the Bill.</p>
